# Compliance research: an Indian saree app with AI try-on

**Decision this research serves:** what legal, tax and platform-policy obligations an own-inventory Indian saree app with AI try-on on shopper photos must meet, and which of them need a lawyer or accountant.

**This is research to brief a lawyer and an accountant. It is not legal or tax advice.**

**Evidence basis.** Four digests, two rounds. Read in official text: the DPDP Act and DPDP Rules, the consolidated IT Rules with the synthetic-content amendments, a MeitY draft notice, PIB releases, GST Council and CBIC FAQs and notifications, two RBI directions, Google Play's policy, Apple's guidelines, and OpenRouter's terms and privacy pages. Not read in official text: the September 2025 GST rate notification, the Act's commencement notification, the E-Commerce Rules 2020 and their 2026 amendment, the Legal Metrology rules, the income-tax cash limit, and Google's Gemini policies.

## Executive summary

**Nothing found blocks the product. Three things must be designed in from the start, and four questions go to professionals before launch.**

Design in from the start:

1. **Consent and deletion for shopper photos.** A standalone itemised notice and affirmative, withdrawable consent before upload; erasure on withdrawal, including at the AI providers; the shop stays liable for every provider in the chain [1][2]. Apple separately requires explicit permission before sharing personal data "with third-party AI" [9]. Most DPDP duties commence about 13 May 2027 [3], but both app stores require the consent and deletion mechanics today [8][9].
2. **An age rule.** Under-18s need verifiable parental consent and may not be tracked or shown targeted ads, with no exemption for retail [1][2]. The simplest product answer is adults-only try-on.
3. **Tax and price as per-product data.** Each product needs its own tax code, fibre and rate, with rates editable by effective date [5][6]; from 1 January 2027 any discount claim needs the 30-day lowest prior price behind it [7].

For professionals:

1. **Accountant: saree GST.** The official 2017 textile FAQ says sarees, embroidered or not, are fabric and the value test does not apply, which gives 5% at any price [5]. Nothing official after the September 2025 changes was found either way, and pre-stitched sarees and stitched blouses fall under the garment chapters with the Rs 2,500 threshold [6].
2. **Lawyer: do the AI-image labelling rules cover the shop?** The rules bind "intermediaries"; whether a shop generating images for a shopper's private viewing is one is unsettled [4].
3. **Lawyer: photos of other people, and how age must be checked.** The Act has no express mechanism for either [1].
4. **Lawyer: the interim regime.** Which older privacy rules apply to photos until May 2027 was not read at source.

## 1. Shopper photos: data protection

**Status.** DPDP Rules, 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E), 13 November 2025) were read at source: some rules took effect on publication, consent-manager rules about 13 November 2026, and notice, security, breach, retention, children, rights and transfer rules about 13 May 2027; whether the day is the 13th or 14th is unresolved [2][3]. The Data Protection Board is established; no member appointments were found (secondary). A reported proposal would shorten the timeline to 12 months (secondary).

| Obligation | Status |
|---|---|
| Standalone, plain-language, itemised notice before upload, with links to withdraw, exercise rights and complain [1][2] | Clear |
| Affirmative consent for the photo, specific to try-on, not bundled [1] | Clear |
| Notice available in English or a scheduled Indian language at the user's option [2] | Clear on the duty; how many languages to offer needs a lawyer |
| Withdrawal as easy as consent; on withdrawal erase the photo and generated images and make processors erase [1][2] | Clear |
| No reuse of photos for training, ads or analytics without separate consent [1] | Clear in principle |
| Erase once the purpose is served, but keep "personal data, associated traffic data and other logs" for one year [2] | Unsettled: how these combine for a photo needs a lawyer |
| Security: encryption, access control, access logs kept one year, backups, a security clause in each processor contract [2] | Clear |
| Breach: tell affected users without delay; tell the Board without delay, with a detailed report in 72 hours [2] | Clear |
| Published contact, grievance response within 90 days, rights requests including a list of processors the photo went to [2] | Clear |
| Under-18s: verifiable parental consent; no tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted ads [1][2] | Duty clear; what age check suffices is unsettled |
| Overseas transfer permitted unless the destination is notified as restricted; none reported [1][2] | Clear on the rule; list status is secondary |
| A valid contract with each processor; the shop stays responsible for what they do [1] | Whether an aggregator's standard terms suffice needs a lawyer |
| Someone else's photo: the personal-use exemption protects the uploader, not the shop [1] | Unsettled |

**Penalties:** up to Rs 250 crore for security failures and Rs 200 crore for breach-notification or children's failures [1].

## 2. AI-generated images

- **Unlawful synthetic images** (nudity, sexually explicit, privacy-invasive or deceptive depictions of a real person) must be blocked; this is an express duty for intermediaries and an offence in any case [4]. Google Play bans "undress" apps [8].
- **Labelling.** The IT Rules require a visible label and embedded, non-removable provenance metadata on synthetic content from intermediaries offering such tools [4]. A shop is arguably not an intermediary for images it generates itself for a user's private view, and arguably is one because it processes a user-supplied photo; both readings are open [4]. A MeitY draft of April 2026 would require the label to be continuous and clearly visible [4].
- **Advertising.** A self-regulatory advertising rule reported to start 1 January 2027 asks for a label where AI content materially influences consumers (secondary) [13]. Official guidelines say a disclaimer cannot correct a misleading image [12].
- **Practical reading:** label every try-on image as AI-generated, keep provenance metadata, never use a try-on image as the only depiction of colour or design, and accept mismatch as a return ground. This satisfies the stricter reading at low cost.

## 3. Tax

- **Sarees.** Official 2017 CBEC textile FAQ: sarees, whether or not embroidered, are fabric, classified by fibre, and the value-based rate test does not apply [5]. The September 2025 Council recommendation applies the Rs 2,500 per-piece test to the garment chapters (5% up to Rs 2,500, 18% above) [6]. No official statement on sarees after September 2025 and no advance ruling was found. Pre-stitched sarees and stitched blouses fall on the garment side; a saree sold with a stitched blouse may need two invoice lines. **Accountant to confirm.**
- **Verified in official material:** no tax collection at source for sales on one's own website; tax codes of 4 digits up to Rs 5 crore turnover and 6 above; e-invoicing is for business-to-business invoices; dynamic QR on consumer invoices only above Rs 500 crore turnover [16].
- **From a 2018 official FAQ:** tax follows the state where delivery ends; returns need a credit note linked to the original invoice; shipping charged to the customer forms part of the taxable value [16].
- **Not established:** tax on COD fees; the credit-note deadline (30 November, secondary only).

| Per product: country of origin, all-inclusive price, return, refund, exchange, delivery and payment terms [7] | Clear |

| "No return" cannot apply to defective, wrong or not-as-described items; worn-item and refused-COD rules are policy choices to disclose | Needs a lawyer |
| "Silk", "handloom", Silk Mark and regional names need evidence behind them | Governing texts unread; needs a lawyer |

## 5. Payments and cash on delivery

- Refunds go to the original payment method unless the payer asks otherwise [10].
- The shop must not store card numbers; card handling stays with the payment aggregator [10].
- Failed payments auto-reverse within set days; the order system must not ship or double-charge on a late confirmation [10].
- COD refunds have no source to return to, so bank or UPI details and consent must be collected. COD cash from the courier needs its own reconciliation.
- Cap COD order value well under Rs 2 lakh (cash-receipt limit; statute unread).

## 6. App stores, OpenRouter and model providers

**Stores (read at source; Play policy effective 29 October 2026, Apple guidelines updated 16 October 2026).**

- In-app control to report a generated image, feeding a queue the admin can see: required by Play; Apple's rule names "AI content creation apps" but is silent on privately viewed output [8][9].
- Explicit permission before sharing personal data with third-party AI (Apple 5.1.2(i)); developer responsible for third-party AI (Play) [8][9].
- System photo picker, not broad gallery access [8][9].
- Account deletion in the app, and at a public web address for Play, removing photos and generated images [8][9].
- Privacy policy, Play Data safety form and Apple privacy labels must name photo collection and the AI services receiving photos (Apple label detail unread).
- Target audience declared as adults; Play's child-safety certification applies only to social, chat and dating apps [8].

**OpenRouter (read at source).**

- Prompts and outputs are not stored unless logging is switched on; flagged child-abuse content is the exception [14].
- Routing can be limited to zero-retention or no-data-collection endpoints; which image models qualify could not be determined [14].
- The customer warrants it has consent for all content sent and must pass model terms down to end users [14].
- No availability guarantee; credit purchases carry a 5.5% fee and credits expire after 365 days [14].

**Model providers.** OpenAI bars use of a person's likeness without consent and sexualisation of under-18s; a moderation block should not be retried on the same input [15]. Google's Gemini policies were not readable.

## Cross-dimension insights

1. **The stores enforce today what the DPDP Rules will require in 2027.** Consent before sharing with AI and full deletion are launch requirements regardless of the commencement date [8][9].
2. **Admin freedom over prompts and models has legal limits.** An admin-editable prompt could produce prohibited output [4][8], and an admin-selected model could route photos to an unchecked provider [14]. The admin needs guardrails: an approved-model list with data policy recorded, locked safety wording in prompts, and logging settings that cannot be switched on by accident.
3. **Labelling try-on images serves four rules at once:** the IT Rules if they apply, the advertising rule, the misleading-advertisement guidelines and the return-dispute position [4][12][13].
4. **Tax depends on catalogue data the try-on research also wants.** Fibre, weave and whether a piece is stitched decide the tax code [5][6]; the same attributes help search and try-on prompts.
5. **Adults-only try-on removes the hardest unsettled question** (age verification and parental consent) at the cost of excluding teenagers from one feature.

## Contrary evidence

No red-team pass was run. One advisory guide dissents from the fabric reading for sarees (round 1, secondary). A reported proposal would bring the DPDP dates forward (secondary).

## Recommendations

1. **PRD, consent and deletion:** itemised consent screen naming the AI services, recorded consent, one-tap withdrawal, deletion of photos and results at the shop and providers, account deletion in app and on web [1][2][8][9]. Basis: official text.
2. **PRD, try-on policy:** adults only; shopper confirms the photo is of herself; a route for a depicted person to demand removal; terms forbidding others' photos [1][14][15]. Basis: official text plus interpretation; lawyer to confirm.
3. **PRD, AI governance in admin:** approved-model list with each model's data policy and terms recorded; zero-retention routing where available; locked safety wording; report queue; non-retry on moderation blocks with a fallback model; audit log [4][8][14][15].
4. **PRD, catalogue and tax:** per-product tax code, fibre, stitched flag and rate with effective dates; per-piece threshold computed on the discounted pre-tax price; two invoice lines for saree plus stitched blouse; credit notes on returns [5][6][16]. Accountant to confirm rates.
5. **PRD, consumer rules:** disclosures, grievance clocks, 30-day price history, dark-pattern checklist, garment declarations, evidence fields behind silk and handloom claims [7][11].
6. **PRD, payments:** refunds to source, no card storage, COD cap, COD refund details, separate COD reconciliation, safe handling of late payment confirmations [10].
7. **Before launch:** one session each with a lawyer and an accountant on the open questions below.
