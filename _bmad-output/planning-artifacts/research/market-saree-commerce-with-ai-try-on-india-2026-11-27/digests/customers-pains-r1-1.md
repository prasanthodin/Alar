## Contradictions

- **Fashion return rate.** Unicommerce's CEO (via ClickPost, https://www.clickpost.ai/blog/fashion-ecommerce-returns-statistics-india) gives 20-25% customer returns and a 17% June 2026 benchmark; LocalCircles-attributed text gives 30-35% for apparel and footwear; Razorpay (https://razorpay.com/blog/business-banking/the-true-cost-of-cod-returns-for-indian-d2c-brands/) gives 25-35%; Base.com (https://base.com/en-IN/blog/?p=22444) gives fashion RTO of 25-40%; Meesho's prospectus is reported at about 7.6% customer returns. Most of the gap is definitional (customer returns vs COD RTO vs both combined; marketplace vs D2C), but the sources do not state definitions consistently.
- **COD RTO level.** Meesho 22.3% (FY25 prospectus, via ClickPost/Finshots) vs ClickPost D2C network 37.51%. Plausibly marketplace vs small D2C brands, not confirmed.
- **Who the saree buyer is.** Fashion Value Chain's small survey says Tier-1 has higher saree spending power; CMR says Tier-II total online spend is near parity with Tier-I; Meesho data says volume is about 88% outside the top 8 cities; Upstox says rural is 60% of saree volume while urban drives revenue. These are compatible as "volume is non-metro, value per order is metro", but no single source measures both for sarees online.
- **Try-on effect.** Vendor claims (DRESSX: +50% conversion, -40% returns) vs peer-reviewed evidence that the effect is contingent and can be negative when used improperly, and that personalised try-on beats non-personalised only when good promotional photos are absent (Yang and Xiong, https://jmis-web.org/articles/1444). The latter matters directly: the planned full-length zoomable imagery could reduce the marginal value of the try-on.
- **Privacy.** Okta/Statista shows Indians unusually willing to share data with AI; PwC and CMR show 76-82% voicing privacy concern. Stated concern and actual sharing diverge; neither measures photo upload to a shopping app.
- **Colour accuracy on saree.com.** Trustpilot reviewers on the same page report both "total mismatch" and colour matching the photo (https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/saree.com?page=3).

## Looked for, not found

- Any survey of online saree buyers with age, income, language or device breakdown. Only one 112-person urban survey surfaced.
- Saree-specific or ethnic-wear-specific return or RTO rates from a primary source. Only a single seller anecdote.
- A percentage breakdown of return reasons for Indian fashion, and any breakdown for sarees. The only reason split found is a US seller survey.
- Complaints specifically about border, pallu or full design not being visible in photos. Complaints found are about the delivered item differing from the photo (colour, fabric, quality, wrong item), not about what the photos omit. No direct evidence yet for the full-length-view feature's problem statement.
- Complaints about fake zari or Silk Mark specifically. Only generic "silk is synthetic" evidence, mostly from seller blogs.
- Reddit, Quora or app-store review threads on saree buying (search did not surface them).
- Independent (non-vendor) evidence that photo-based AI try-on reduces returns, in any geography. Peer-reviewed evidence found concerns fit tools and virtual fitting rooms from 2018-2019.
- Any evidence on try-on for sarees or other draped, unstitched garments.
- Usage or adoption data for apparel try-on among Indian shoppers, including Google's Dec 2025 launch.
- Any India-specific measure of willingness to upload a full-length personal photo to a shopping app, or differences by gender, age or city tier.
- Live-commerce or YouTube-seller data specific to sarees.
