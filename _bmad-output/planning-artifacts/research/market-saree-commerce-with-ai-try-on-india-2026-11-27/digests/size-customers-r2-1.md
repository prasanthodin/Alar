## Contradictions

1. **Market size.** Redseer's US$12 billion (CY24) is well above the Rs 61,700 crore (FY25) headline figure, and IMARC's US$5.76 billion (2024) is below both. Rupee conversion of the dollar figures was not done with a retrieved exchange rate; on any plausible rate Redseer's figure is around or above Rs 1 lakh crore, which matches the top of round 1's range. These are different estimates, likely with different scope. Report both; do not average.
2. **Regional leader.** Technopak and CRISIL place the South first at about 47–49% of the saree market; IMARC reportedly gives the largest share to the North (via a secondary site, https://www.firstresort.in/blogs/research/india-ethnic-wear-market-2026).
3. **Technopak FY20 organised figures.** A Business Standard extract gives the organised saree market as about Rs 12,500 crore and says the South contributes "38 per cent of it" worth Rs 8,800 crore. Rs 8,800 crore is 70% of Rs 12,500 crore, not 38%. Rs 8,800 crore is 38% of the Rs 23,200 crore South market, so the likeliest reading is "38% of the South's saree market is organised". If the Rs 12,500 crore national organised figure is right, the South would be about 70% of organised saree sales, not 50%. Unresolved without the Technopak page.
4. **Return to origin by payment mode.** Meesho-derived: about 22% COD vs 2.7% prepaid (FY2025). ClickPost network: 37.51% vs 5.28%. A second Meesho reading: about 24% vs 3.7% (period not seen).
5. **LocalCircles sample size.** Over 102,000 responses from 327+ districts (Moneylife) vs 1.2 lakh consumers (The Week). The LocalCircles release itself could not be opened.
6. **Fashion return range.** ClickPost separates customer returns (17–25%) from total reverse flow including COD rejection (25–40%). Round 1's "17–35%" matches neither.
