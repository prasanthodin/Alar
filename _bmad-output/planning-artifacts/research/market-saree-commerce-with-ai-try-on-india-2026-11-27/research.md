# Market research: saree commerce with AI try-on, India

**Decision this research serves:** whether and how to enter the Indian online saree market with an own-inventory, mobile-first app led by AI try-on and a full-saree viewer; which segment and price band to target; and whether try-on is a real differentiator.

**Evidence basis (read first).** Six research digests, two rounds. Researchers could not open most source pages from this session, so most claims rest on search-result extracts of the named page. Only sources [1] and [5] were read in full by the lead. Confidence is marked per claim; "high" is rare by design.

## Executive summary

**Enter, but not at the bottom of the market, and do not treat "has AI try-on" as the moat.**

1. **The category is large, fragmented and badly served online.** Redseer sizes sarees at about US$12 billion (CY24), 60–65% unbranded, and says online discovery suffers from poor categorisation, inconsistent quality and minimal trust [1] (high). Other size estimates disagree by a factor of two [2][3], and nobody publishes a credible online share.
2. **Online fashion growth sits below Rs 800 per item [5] (high), and that ground is taken.** Meesho serves it with an average order near Rs 274 [9] and about three-quarters of orders on cash on delivery [8], where roughly one COD order in five fails delivery [10]. An own-inventory newcomer paying per try-on image cannot win there. The evidence points to a mid-to-premium band, with South India (about half the market [4]) as the natural first region. This targeting is an inference from the evidence, not a finding.
3. **Try-on on the shopper's own photo, for sarees, is not offered by any large Indian platform found, but it is not unclaimed.** Google's try-on launched in India on 3 December 2025 without sarees in its listed categories [17][18]; Myntra was piloting own-photo try-on in July 2026, saree coverage unknown [20]; Meesho's saree try-on is seller-side on stock models [21]. Yet a crowd of small apps and API vendors already claim own-photo saree try-on [22][23][24][25], with no independent quality test of any of them. The differentiator available is **drape fidelity that buyers trust**, and the window before a large platform adds sarees is unknown.

**Biggest caveat.** No evidence was found that try-on raises saree sales or cuts returns in India: only vendor claims [16], and academic work finding the effect is contingent and weaker where good product photos exist [15]. No evidence was found that buyers complain about not seeing the whole saree. Both differentiators are untested hypotheses.

## 1. Market size and growth

- **Size is disputed; do not average.** Redseer: about US$12 billion in CY24, 100+ million SKUs, 60–65% unbranded and a further 25–30% regional brands [1] (high that Redseer says this). A Technopak-linked projection: about Rs 61,700 crore for FY25 at 5–7% a year [2] (medium, a pre-FY25 projection, not an actual). IMARC: US$5.76 billion in 2024 [3] (low, report seller). Scope definitions were not visible.
- **Organised share:** about 40–41% per Technopak material [4] (low-medium, chart not confirmed) against Redseer's 60–65% unbranded [1].
- **Online share of sarees: not found.** All Indian retail is about 8% online in CY2025 [6] (medium). An implied 35% online share from IMARC is not credible against that.
- **Region:** South India is about 49.3% of the national saree market in FY2024 [4] (medium). Round 1's "half of organised sales" wording was wrong; the figure is share of the total market.
- **Price band:** the fastest-growing cluster in online fashion is below Rs 800 average selling price; mid and premium track category growth, and evidence of acceleration at higher prices "remains limited" [5] (high). No saree-specific volume by price band was found.
- **Listed-company signal:** Sai Silks (Kalamandir), the listed saree-first retailer, traded near Rs 90 in November 2026 against a 52-week high of Rs 223 [37] (medium). Saree retail at scale is not an easy business.

## 2. Customers and behaviour

- **The mass online value buyer is outside the big cities.** Meesho: 174.43 million of 198.77 million FY25 transacting users were outside the top 8 cities, about 88% [7] (medium, via press quoting the prospectus).
- **Cash on delivery dominates that segment but is falling:** 75.09% of Meesho's shipped orders in the quarter to June 2025, about 72% in the first half of FY26 [8] (medium).
- **Saree-specific buyer data is thin.** No credible profile of who buys sarees online by age, income or language was found. Regional taste differences (colour, fabric, occasion) are described qualitatively by Redseer [1].
- **Privacy:** no India data on willingness to upload one's own photo to a shopping app. General surveys show strong concern: 82% of respondents rank data protection as key to trust [35] (medium, not about photos).

## 3. Pain points and unmet needs

- **Trust and "not as shown" are the evidenced pains.** Redseer names inconsistent quality, limited availability and lack of product trust [1] (high). LocalCircles, January 2025, all categories: 48% of online shoppers were stuck with a wrong product at least once in 12 months and 20% received a fake [13] (medium; self-selected panel).
- **Saree complaints are about colour, fabric and authenticity** ("pure silk" that buyers say is synthetic), mostly against small standalone sites, 2018–2025 [14] (low; individual complaints, nothing quantified).
- **No evidence that buyers complain about not seeing the border, pallu or full length.** This is the problem the full-saree viewer targets. Absence here may reflect what people write complaints about rather than what they want, so it is untested, not disproved.
- **Returns:** customer-initiated fashion returns 17–25%; 25–40% once COD doorstep rejections are included [12] (low-medium, vendor blog). Size and fit drive most apparel returns; sarees are unsized, so colour and "differs from description" are the transferable reasons. No saree-specific return data found.
- **Does try-on fix this?** Vendor claims of large conversion gains and return reductions are observational and self-interested [16] (low). Field experiments (not India) found virtual fitting can raise sales and cut returns but can backfire, and a personalised version beat a generic one only where ordinary product photos were unavailable [15] (medium).

## 4. Competitive landscape

**Who sells sarees online.** Horizontal marketplaces (Myntra, Flipkart, Amazon, Meesho, Ajio, Nykaa Fashion); saree-led retailers and brands (Sai Silks: FY25 revenue about Rs 1,462 crore, sarees 71.5% of it, gross margin 41.8% [27]; Suta: FY25 revenue Rs 73.5 crore, small loss [28]); and WhatsApp, Instagram and live-video sellers (evidence old and anecdotal).

**Who offers try-on.**

| Player | What it is | Sarees on shopper's own photo? | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Shopping | Own-photo try-on, India launch 3 Dec 2025; tops, bottoms, dresses, jackets, shoes [17][18] | Not listed; a competing vendor says not covered [19] | High on launch; low on saree exclusion |
| Myntra | "Try it on Me", own photo, piloting or rolling out July 2026; expansion gated on A/B results [20] | Unknown | Medium |
| Meesho | Saree try-on with Google Cloud for suppliers, on stock models photographed in white sarees [21] | No (seller-side) | High for 2024; no later update found |
| Flipkart, Amazon India | Beauty try-on only; Amazon apparel try-on reported US-only [36] | None found | Medium (absence) |
| Small apps (Looksy AI, fit., SareeStage, Changing Room and others) | Own-photo try-on, some saree-specific, some overlaying marketplace listings [22][23] | Claimed | Medium that they exist; quality unevidenced |
| API vendors (TryOnCloud, TryVastra, AI Vastra) | White-label try-on claiming Indian ethnic wear; TryOnCloud about Rs 6.50–9 per try-on [24][25] | Claimed | Low; no named customers, inconsistent pricing and ratings |

**Why sarees are hard.** An academic benchmark on South Asian garments cites complex draping, asymmetric layering and high deformation, under-represented in standard try-on datasets [26] (medium, abstract only). Meesho and Google did not use a generic garment-transfer model; they needed purpose-shot model photos [21].

**How sarees are shown today.** Not established. Marketplace image rules, as described by seller-tool vendors, ask for model-worn shots with the drape and pallu visible. Whether any seller offers a full-length zoomable view was not tested; product pages need direct inspection.

## 5. Go-to-market and pricing

- **Margins:** Sai Silks gross margin 41.8% in FY25 [27] (high). D2C saree margins not found.
- **Marketing cost:** Suta reportedly spends 20–25% of revenue on marketing [29] (low). Fashion D2C acquisition cost Rs 340–1,420 per customer depending on scale [30] (low).
- **COD risk:** Meesho delivery success FY2025: 97.28% prepaid against 77.70% COD [10] (low-medium, third-party reading of the filing). On a D2C logistics network: 37.51% of COD orders returned to origin against 5.28% prepaid; fashion 21–32% [11] (low-medium, vendor data). Some brands now charge a Rs 99 COD commitment fee [38] (low-medium).
- **Payment cost:** Razorpay standard 2% plus GST on the fee, 2.36% effective [31] (medium; pricing page itself not read).
- **Shipping:** roughly Rs 50–90 per 500 g forward, COD fee extra [34] (low). No reliable reverse-shipping figure found.
- **GST:** from 22 September 2025, apparel is 5% up to Rs 2,500 per piece and 18% on the whole value above it [32][33] (high on slabs). **Whether an unstitched saree is taxed as fabric (5% at any price) or as apparel under this threshold is contradicted between secondary sources and unresolved.** This changes pricing above Rs 2,500 and needs an accountant.

## Cross-dimension insights

1. **Try-on cost against order value decides the price band.** At a vendor's Rs 6.50–9 per try-on [24], several try-ons per browsing session are a real cost against a Rs 274 order [9] and negligible against a Rs 2,500 one. Combined with COD failure rates in the value segment [10][11], the economics favour mid-to-premium and prepaid-leaning buyers, even though growth is fastest at the bottom [5].
2. **The evidenced pain is trust in colour, fabric and authenticity, not visualisation of oneself.** Try-on that changes the saree's colour or zari would worsen the exact problem buyers complain about. The full-saree viewer and accurate imagery may address the evidenced pain more directly than try-on does.
3. **The competitive gap is quality, and it is temporary.** Large platforms have the general technology and have skipped sarees, apparently because drape is hard [21][26]. That is both the opening and the risk.
4. **Redseer's unmet needs are not the founder's two features.** It names regional curation, vernacular content, localised catalogues and quality assurance [1]. These are candidates for the product alongside try-on.

## Contrary evidence

No red-team pass was run (off at standard effort). Counter-evidence that surfaced anyway: try-on benefits are contingent [15]; tier 2 and 3 shoppers are reported sceptical of virtual try-on (trade press, low); the listed saree retailer has lost most of its market value in a year [37].

## Recommendations

1. **Product brief, target segment:** position at mid-to-premium (provisionally Rs 1,500–5,000), South India first, prepaid-leaning. Rests on an inference from [4][5][9][10]; medium at best.
2. **Product brief, differentiation:** state the moat as "saree imagery you can trust" (colour-true, full design, faithful drape), with try-on as one expression of it. Rests on [1][13][14] (medium to low) and the competitor table.
3. **Before the PRD:** run the try-on feasibility test on real sarees and real photos, including the existing vendors [22][24][25] as candidates, since none has been independently tested. Rests on absence of evidence, stated as such.
4. **PRD inputs:** COD controls (commitment fee, prepaid incentives) as requirements [10][11][38]; per-user try-on quotas and a cost ceiling [24]; explicit consent, retention and deletion for photos [35]; regional and language curation [1].
5. **Architecture constraint:** treat the try-on provider as replaceable; vendors are young, pricing is unstable [24][25].
6. **Validate demand directly:** interview or survey saree buyers on both features before building, because public evidence for either is absent.
