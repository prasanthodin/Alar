# PRD Quality Review — Alar (prd-saree-commerce-2026-11-27)

Reviewed: `prd.md` and `addendum.md`. Rubric: `.claude/skills/bmad-prd/assets/prd-validation-checklist.md`. Stakes: launch; chain-top (feeds UX, architecture, epics and stories).

Finding counts: 1 critical, 11 high, 21 medium, 7 low.

## Overall verdict

The PRD has a real thesis (remove the dealer; give a phone buyer the confidence of a shop through a named Weaver, a Full saree view and Try-on), honest scope, and unusually specific privacy, AI-control and tax requirements. It is not yet safe to hand to architecture or epics: the money-and-stock lifecycle contradicts itself across FR-18, FR-19, the Glossary and the addendum; the inventory model (Design, Variant, Piece) is ambiguous; and a production shop's plumbing is missing or one line deep (notifications, order and return states, payment failure, retention of Shopper photos, account and Admin security). Many FRs also rest on adjectives ("at once", "roughly when", "far enough") where a story writer needs a bound.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

Decisions are stated as decisions: prepaid only (FR-18), adults only (FR-9), Try-on ships only through a gate with a named fallback ("Alar launches without Try-on and the feature waits", §6.3), what is and is not a Server-driven screen (§4.7). The Open Questions in §10 are genuinely open, and the ⚖ markers in §8 put legal uncertainty where it belongs.

Two things weaken it. First, several Open Questions are inputs to FRs that are written as if settled (return period in FR-23, limits in FR-14 and FR-42, languages in FR-8, shipping charge in FR-5), and nothing says which of them block which piece of work. Second, the hosting decision is left open (Open Question 7) while NFR-4 and NFR-8 make promises that depend on it, and the addendum itself records that the free tier "can reclaim idle servers and carries no service guarantee". There are no `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts at all, although real tensions exist (see the consent versus Model route finding below).

### Findings
- **high** Recovery targets do not protect payments, and hosting is undecided (NFR-8, NFR-4, Open Question 7, addendum "NFR-8") — "The database is backed up daily" allows up to a day of paid Orders to be lost, which breaks NFR-4 ("no payment without an Order or an automatic refund"). Backups cover "the database" only, not product photos, Full saree view images, invoices or Shopper photos. The 4-hour rebuild is promised on hosting that is not chosen. *Fix:* state the maximum acceptable data loss for Orders and payments (for example, 15 minutes or less, or "any Order lost in a restore is rebuilt from the payment service's records"), include stored files in the backup scope, and settle Open Question 7 before architecture: real payments do not run on a tier that can reclaim the server.
- **medium** Open Questions are not tied to the work they block (§10) — Questions 2, 3, 4 and 5 each feed a specific FR. *Fix:* add to each question the FRs it blocks and the latest point it can be answered (for example, "4 blocks FR-23; needed before the thin slice").
- **medium** No `[NOTE FOR PM]` at the real tensions (whole document) — the consent notice versus route changes, FR-50's scope, and sign-in at checkout versus "no forced sign-ups" are silent. *Fix:* add a callout at each, or resolve them.

## Substance over theater — strong

There is little furniture. The Vision could not be swapped into another shop's PRD: it names the dealer, the family, and the three specific means of confidence. There are no decorative personas; Lakshmi exists to carry UJ-1 and UJ-2. The NFRs mostly carry numbers or mechanisms (3 seconds, 95% in 60 seconds, 24 hours, 72 hours, second factor, one-year logs). The Admin AI controls (FR-39 to FR-46) are specific in a way that shows thought: approved list with recorded data policy, uneditable safety wording, test set before publishing, uneditable history.

### Findings
- **low** NFR-15 is the one boilerplate NFR (§7) — "Text is readable at large system font sizes and every action is reachable by screen reader" has no level or bound. *Fix:* name a target (for example, WCAG 2.1 AA on the website; text scales to 200% without loss of content on Android).

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The thesis is clear and the features follow from it: Weaver provenance (FR-4), evidence-backed claims (FR-38), Full saree view (FR-6), honest Try-on (FR-12). The counter-metrics are real and aimed at the right temptations (SM-C1 to SM-C3). The thin slice and the Try-on gate in §6.3 order the work by risk, not by ease.

The metrics are where it slips: the second primary metric cannot be measured with the FRs as written, and two secondary metrics appear to pull against each other.

### Findings
- **high** Primary metric SM-2 has no way to be collected, and no FR covers measurement at all (§9) — SM-2 needs a "Matched what I saw" rating from buyers, but no FR asks a buyer anything after delivery. SM-4 and SM-5 need usage measurement, and no FR or NFR covers analytics or says what tracking is allowed (FR-15 forbids only Shopper photos in analytics). *Fix:* add an FR: after delivery, a Shopper is asked to rate "matched what I saw" from 1 to 5 for each Design in the Order; and an FR or NFR stating which events are measured (Design-page views, Try-on requests, Orders) and under what consent.
- **medium** SM-5 and SM-6 are probably incompatible (§9) — 30% of Design-page visitors using Try-on, with Try-on spend under Rs 25 per Order, implies a very low cost per Try-on result unless a large share of visitors buy. For example, if 2 in 100 Design-page visitors order, 30 Try-on users per 2 Orders leaves under Rs 2 per Shopper for all her results. This is my arithmetic on assumed conversion, not a figure from the PRD. *Fix:* work the numbers once with an expected conversion rate and price per image from the Try-on test, then reset SM-5, SM-6 or the per-Shopper limit so they agree.
- **low** SM-1 "Validates FR-1 to FR-20" is a count of activity (§9) — acceptable for a new shop, but it does not test the thesis; SM-2 and SM-3 do. *Fix:* none required; consider making SM-3 primary alongside SM-2.

## Done-ness clarity — thin

The best FRs here are very good: FR-19's three consequences, FR-18's "confirmed by the payment service to Alar's server, not by the Shopper's device alone", FR-38's "cannot be published while its evidence field is empty", FR-32's "Unknown Blocks are skipped". A story writer can test those.

But the set is uneven. The money and stock path contradicts itself. Roughly a third of the FRs lean on an adjective at the point where the test would be. And the Admin order FRs (FR-44, FR-45, FR-47, FR-48, FR-49) are a single sentence with no consequences, though they carry the Owner's whole daily job.

### Findings
- **critical** The payment and stock lifecycle is contradictory and has no failure path (FR-16, FR-18, FR-19, Glossary "Order", UJ-1 edge case, addendum "FR-19") — The Glossary says an Order is "a paid purchase" and FR-18 says "An Order exists only after the payment is confirmed". The addendum says the commerce system "reserves stock when the order is placed", which in that system is before payment. FR-19 accepts paying for a Piece that is already gone ("payment succeeded but whose Piece is gone is refunded"), while UJ-1 says she "is told before money is taken, or refunded". Nothing says whether stock is held while a Shopper pays, for how long, or when it is released. Nothing covers a failed, abandoned or pending payment (UPI payments can stay pending), a Shopper retrying, or a cart item that sold out or changed price before checkout. *Fix:* add one FR for the hold: "When payment begins, the Pieces are held for N minutes; a hold that ends without a confirmed payment releases them"; state that the refund in FR-19 is the exception for confirmations that arrive after the hold ends; add consequences for failed, pending and abandoned payments (what the Shopper sees, whether she can retry, when the cart is restored); add a consequence to FR-16 for cart items that are sold out or repriced. Reconcile the Glossary: either an unpaid checkout has its own term, or "Order" has states.
- **high** Order, shipping and Return states are not defined, and the Admin side is one line (FR-21 to FR-25, FR-47, FR-48, UJ-2) — "from payment to delivery" names no states. FR-47 says only "handle cancellations and Returns". UJ-2 says the refund follows "When the saree passes inspection", but no FR says what happens when it fails inspection, who pays return shipping, how long the refund takes, or whether a reason other than the two "always accepted" ones can be refused. A delivery that fails and comes back (FR-25 "undelivered Piece") has no refund rule. FR-24 "unless she asks otherwise" names no alternative. FR-48 allows a part refund with no rule for the amount or the credit note. *Fix:* list the Order states and Return states with what moves each one and who sees what; give FR-47 consequences (approve or refuse a Return with a reason the Shopper sees; record inspection result; refund started within N days of a passed inspection); add a rule for failed inspection, for undelivered Orders (full refund once the Piece is back, or re-delivery once), and for return shipping cost; either define the alternative in FR-24 or delete the clause.
- **high** Many FRs put an adjective where the test should be (FR-2, FR-6, FR-7, FR-11, FR-15, FR-17, FR-19, FR-21, NFR-1, NFR-7, NFR-9) — examples: "offers the nearest filters" (FR-2); "zooms far enough to see weave detail" (FR-6); "too small to zoom usefully ... with the minimum size stated" with no size (FR-7); "accepted immediately", "times out" with no time, "in neutral words" (FR-11); "as easily as she gave it" (FR-15); "roughly when" (FR-17); "told at once" (FR-19); "with what happens next" (FR-21); "usable within 3 seconds" (NFR-1); "without delay" (NFR-7); "above a set rate", "nears its cap" (NFR-9). *Fix:* give each a bound or an observable: for example, FR-6 "at full zoom, individual threads of the border are distinguishable; the source image is at least N pixels on the long side" (and use the same N in FR-7); FR-11 "the request is acknowledged within 2 seconds; the primary model is abandoned after N seconds"; FR-15 "withdrawal takes no more steps than giving consent, from the account screen"; FR-17 "shows an earliest and latest delivery date"; FR-19 "told on screen within the same session and by message within 5 minutes"; NFR-9 "alert at 80% of the cap".
- **high** Admin FRs for daily operation have no consequences (FR-44, FR-45, FR-47, FR-48, FR-49; §4.10 has no description) — these cannot be turned into stories without invention. *Fix:* add 2–3 consequences each. For example FR-47: Orders can be filtered by state; a shipping label is created from the Order without retyping the address; marking dispatch needs a tracking number. FR-49: lookup by mobile number or Order number. FR-44: a handled report records what was done; the Owner can remove the reported Try-on result.
- **medium** Try-on limits have no unit, and "quota" is undefined (FR-11, FR-14, FR-42) — FR-11 says "not charged against her quota"; FR-14 says "limit". Nothing says whether the limit is per day, per Design or in total, or whether a request blocked by a safety check counts. *Fix:* use one word; state the unit ("N Try-on results per Shopper per day", N set in FR-42) and that failed requests do not count while blocked ones do or do not.
- **medium** FR-34 carries a metric as a consequence (FR-34) — "The Owner can list a Design in under 15 minutes" is SM-7, not a testable system behaviour. *Fix:* remove it from FR-34, or replace it with what makes it true (for example, "all required fields are on one screen; photos upload in the background").
- **medium** Search and browse lack ordering and matching rules (FR-1, FR-2) — no sort order, no paging rule, no tolerance for spelling. Saree words have many spellings ("Kanchipuram", "Kanjivaram", "Kanchi"), and Open Question 3 may add Tamil. *Fix:* add: default order and the sort choices; search matches common alternative spellings held in an Owner-editable list; whether search works in the launch languages.
- **medium** The delivery region and shipping charge have no rule or owner (FR-5, FR-17, §6.1) — "South India" is never defined as states or pincodes, and "Any shipping charge" has no rule and no Admin FR to set it. *Fix:* name the states; say whether deliverable pincodes come from the shipping service or an Owner-kept list; add an Admin FR for the shipping charge rule (free, flat, or by Order value).
- **medium** The tax invoice is underspecified for a legal document (FR-20) — no invoice number sequence, no seller tax registration, no way to get the invoice again, and the delivery channel is unstated. "A saree sold with a stitched blouse" refers to a product §6.1 excludes ("Sarees only"); FR-3 knows only "blouse piece included or not". *Fix:* add numbering, seller details, and "downloadable from the Order at any time"; mark the stitched-blouse line as later scope or bring stitched blouses into scope.
- **medium** Erasure at AI providers is promised more strongly in the journey than in the FR (UJ-6, FR-15, FR-27, FR-40) — UJ-6: photos "disappear from Alar and from the AI providers"; FR-15: "erasure is requested". Nothing says how a request is recorded, what happens when a provider has no way to erase, or how this relates to the no-retention option in FR-40. *Fix:* align UJ-6 to the FR; add "each erasure request and the provider's response are recorded and visible in FR-49"; state that a model whose provider offers neither no-retention nor erasure cannot be approved.

## Scope honesty — thin

What the PRD says about scope is honest: §2.2, §5 and §6.2 do real work, the `[ASSUMPTION]` tags round-trip to §11, and the conditional status of Try-on is plain. The problem is what it does not say. A production shop needs several capabilities that are neither required nor excluded, so a downstream workflow must guess whether they are in.

Open-items density is high for launch stakes: 8 Open Questions, 6 ⚖ items and about 15 assumption tags. The fast path explains the tags; the Open Questions that set FR values still need answers before the stories that use them.

### Findings
- **high** No requirement covers notifications (UJ-1, FR-11, FR-19, FR-20, FR-21, FR-24, FR-42, NFR-9) — the Shopper "receives" a confirmation and invoice, is "told when the result is ready", "told at once", and the Owner "is alerted", but no channel is named (SMS, WhatsApp, email, app notification). Sign-in is by mobile number only (FR-26), so there may be no email address to send an invoice to. *Fix:* add a feature section "Messages to Shoppers and the Owner" with an FR listing each event (Order confirmed, dispatched, out for delivery, delivered, failed delivery, Return approved, refund started, Try-on ready, limit reset) and its channel; say whether an email address is collected; say how the Owner's alerts arrive; make the wording translatable (NFR-14) and message failures visible to the Owner.
- **high** Try-on has no retention rule and no check on what comes out (FR-8, FR-10, FR-12, FR-15, §5, §8) — FR-8's notice must state "what is kept and for how long", but no FR sets how long Shopper photos and Try-on results are kept; FR-10 implies indefinitely ("reuse a stored Shopper photo"). §5 puts behaviour in Non-goals ("never produces images without clothing") with no FR behind it, and §8 claims "unlawful output blocked (FR-12, FR-41)" although FR-12 only labels and FR-41 only adds wording to the request. FR-11 handles a block by the AI provider on the way in; nothing checks the result before the Shopper sees it. *Fix:* add an FR: Shopper photos and Try-on results are deleted after N days without use, with N in the notice; add an FR: every Try-on result is checked before display and a result that fails is not shown, not stored, and counted in FR-45; move the two §5 behaviour lines into that FR.
- **high** Security requirements stop at encryption (FR-26, NFR-5, NFR-13) — there is no FR for how the Owner signs in to the Admin or recovers access if the second factor is lost (one operator, so a lockout stops the shop); nothing on one-time-code expiry, attempt limits or abuse of code sending (which costs money per message); nothing on session length, a changed mobile number, payment disputes or chargebacks, or automated abuse of checkout and Try-on. NFR-13 forbids "forced sign-ups" while FR-26 requires sign-in for checkout; one of them needs a definition. *Fix:* add FRs for Admin sign-in and recovery, for one-time-code limits (expiry, attempts, sends per number and per device per hour), for a Shopper changing her number, and for the Owner seeing and answering payment disputes; define "forced sign-up" in NFR-13 so that sign-in at checkout is clearly allowed or guest checkout is required.
- **high** FR-50 is both in and out of the first version (FR-50, §6.1, §11, addendum) — §6.1 says "Everything in §4, with FR-50 last"; the tag says "needed before a team joins, not at launch". The addendum records that the commerce system "charges for audit logs and staff role permissions" and leaves buy-or-build open. FR-36 and FR-46 already record "who made it". Architecture cannot size this. *Fix:* decide: move FR-50 to §6.2 with a `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` note and one constraint for architecture ("every Admin action is recorded against a named account so roles can be added later"), or keep it and state the roles and permissions.
- **medium** Discounts are assumed but never required (FR-5, FR-37) — "A discount is shown only with the lowest price of the previous 30 days" and "after any discount" presume a way to discount; no FR lets the Owner create a sale price or a code. *Fix:* add an FR for sale prices (and say whether codes exist), or add a Non-goal "no discount codes at launch; a price change is the only discount".
- **medium** General privacy and marketing consent are absent (§4.6) — consent is specified only for Try-on. Nothing covers consent for promotional messages, what tracking the website and app do, or how long Order and account data are kept. *Fix:* add an FR for marketing-message consent (off by default, withdrawable) and a retention line per data type, or state as Non-goals "no promotional messages and no third-party tracking at launch".
- **medium** No requirement for forcing or prompting an app update (FR-32, §6.1) — FR-32 covers unknown Blocks, but not an old app that can no longer check out safely. *Fix:* add: "The Owner can set a minimum app version; older versions show an update prompt and cannot check out."
- **medium** A person without an account has a right but no route (FR-9, FR-44) — "A person who appears in a Try-on result without agreeing can ask for it to be removed through a published contact", but no Admin FR lets the Owner find and remove another Shopper's photo or result. *Fix:* add to FR-49 or FR-44: the Owner can remove any Shopper photo or Try-on result and record why.
- **medium** Saved Try-on results are implied, not specified (FR-12, FR-15) — "wherever it is shown or saved" and "delete a single ... Try-on result" imply a Shopper can keep, revisit or download results. *Fix:* state whether a Shopper has a list of past Try-on results, and whether she can download one (with the label burned in, per FR-12).
- **medium** Payment reconciliation has no Admin view (NFR-4, NFR-10) — NFR-4 needs a daily reconciliation and NFR-10 says nothing routine needs a command line, but no FR shows the Owner the result or lets her resolve a mismatch. *Fix:* add an Admin FR: a daily list of payments without Orders and Orders without payments, each resolvable by refund or by attaching the payment.
- **medium** Languages cover text but not content (FR-8, NFR-14, Open Question 3) — NFR-14 makes interface text translatable; nothing says whether Design descriptions and Weaver stories are translated or shown in English only. *Fix:* state it either way.
- **low** No availability target (§7) — NFR-9 alerts on downtime but no NFR says how much is acceptable. *Fix:* add a modest target (for example, 99.5% monthly for browsing and checkout) once hosting is chosen.
- **low** Product reviews are neither in nor out (§5) — a shopper-facing shop invites the assumption. *Fix:* add a Non-goal line if excluded.

## Downstream usability — thin

IDs are clean: FR-1 to FR-50, UJ-1 to UJ-6, SM-1 to SM-7, NFR-1 to NFR-15 are contiguous and the cross-references resolve. The Glossary is good on the Try-on and AI vocabulary. Sections mostly stand alone.

The data model that architecture will extract first is the weak point: the three inventory nouns do not fit together, and one consent requirement collides with one Admin capability.

### Findings
- **high** Design, Variant and Piece do not form a consistent inventory model (Glossary, FR-1, FR-16, FR-19, FR-25, FR-35, FR-36, FR-37) — the Glossary says a Piece is "one physical saree of a Design" and a Design has "its own photos, description, price"; FR-35 and FR-36 count stock, price and photos per Variant; FR-37 sets the tax code per Design although FR-35 lets price vary by Variant and FR-37 makes the rate depend on value. "A Piece is never sold twice" (FR-19) and "A Piece marked damaged" (FR-25) treat a Piece as an identified object, while stock is "set and adjust how many" (FR-36), a count. Handloom Pieces of one Design can also differ slightly, which matters to the thesis. *Fix:* decide and state: are Pieces counted or individually tracked (each with its own identifier and condition)? Redefine Piece as "one physical saree of a Variant"; say which of photos, price and tax data live on the Design and which on the Variant; reword FR-19 as "no more Pieces are sold than exist" if Pieces are counted.
- **high** The consent notice names AI services, but the Owner can change the service without a release (FR-8, FR-28, FR-39, FR-40) — FR-8: the photo "is sent to named AI services" and "A changed notice requires fresh acceptance". FR-39 lets the Owner switch model and AI provider at will, and the first AI provider is an aggregator that passes photos on to others. As written, either a route change silently makes the notice untrue, or every route change cancels every Shopper's consent. *Fix:* state the rule: the notice lists every AI provider and underlying model host on the Approved-model list; publishing a Model route that uses a recipient not in the current notice is blocked until a new notice version is published; Shoppers are asked again only when a new recipient is added. Record the recipient of each request so FR-28 can be answered.
- **high** The Design page is declared server-driven but also has fixed, partly legal, content (§4.7, FR-3, FR-30) — §4.7 lists "Design-page layout" among Server-driven screens. FR-3 requires fixed things on that page (entry points "visible without scrolling", the maker's details, consumer-care contact, terms), and FR-30's Blocks (banner, rows, grid, Weaver story, text, image) include none of the Design-page parts. An Owner edit could remove a required disclosure. *Fix:* either take the Design page out of §4.7, or say which parts are fixed and which region the Owner can arrange, and add the Design-page Blocks to FR-30.
- **medium** Server-driven screens have gaps in schedule and fallback (FR-31, FR-32, UJ-5) — a campaign can start "at a set time" but has no end; FR-32's fallback ("the last screen it received") does not cover a first launch with no connection; "category" and "campaign" screens are named in §4.7 but neither word is defined and no FR creates a category. *Fix:* add an end time to FR-31; add "a built-in default home screen is shown if none was ever received"; define Category and Campaign in the Glossary or drop the words.
- **medium** "App" and "website" are used loosely (FR-10, FR-32, FR-33, §4.7) — FR-10 "The app uses the device's own photo picker", FR-33 "already built into the app": it is unclear whether these bind the website. UJ-1 does Try-on on the website. *Fix:* say per FR whether it applies to the website, the Android app or both; add a line to §6.1 that the two offer the same features unless stated.
- **medium** "The maker's name and address" is ambiguous (FR-3) — it could be the Weaver (a Glossary term with "name, place") or the legal manufacturer or seller required by consumer rules. Publishing a weaving family's address is also a privacy question. *Fix:* use "Weaver" if meant, or "the seller's legal name and address", and say what of a Weaver's place is public.

## Shape fit — strong

The shape matches the product: a consumer shop with a single operator. Shopper journeys with a named protagonist carry the buying side; the Owner side is a capability spec with three short journeys, which is right for one operator. Compliance is traced to FRs in §8 without turning the PRD into a regulatory document. Technology is kept in the addendum as intended, and the addendum's mapping of FRs to known technical facts is the kind of note architecture can use.

### Findings
- **low** The addendum's "Design and media assets" section records session events, not requirements (addendum) — "The Mobbin connection failed to connect in this session" and "Image and video prompts are therefore written here" (none are). *Fix:* delete the session notes or move them to the working log; keep only the founder's stated direction.

## Mechanical notes

- **Glossary drift.** "owner" and "shopper" appear in lower case in UJ-3 to UJ-6; "founder" and "Owner" are used for the same person without saying so. "quota" (FR-11) versus "limit" (FR-14, FR-42). "item" and "Order item" (Glossary "Return", FR-20, FR-24) versus Piece. "AI services" (FR-8, Glossary "Try-on consent") versus AI provider. "product" (Glossary "Design", FR-12 "product photos") is fine but "saree" and Design alternate (FR-6, FR-7). "full-length image" (FR-6, FR-7) versus "full-length photo" (Shopper photo): two different things with near-identical names.
- **Terms used but not in the Glossary.** payment service, shipping service, launch region, notice, review queue, sample set, campaign, category, stitched, tax code, return period, cart.
- **ID continuity.** FR, UJ, SM and NFR numbering is contiguous and unique. Cross-references resolve. Weak references: §8 "unlawful output blocked (FR-12, FR-41)" (neither blocks output); SM-1 "Validates FR-1 to FR-20" is a range, not a trace.
- **Journeys to FRs.** UJ-1 to UJ-6 are each realized by at least one feature. FRs with no journey: FR-22, FR-26 to FR-29, FR-50 (acceptable). UJ-4's "sees reported results ... rolls back if it is worse" is covered by FR-39, FR-41, FR-43, FR-44.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip.** Every inline `[ASSUMPTION]` appears in §11 and every §11 entry appears inline. No gaps.
- **UJ protagonists.** UJ-1 and UJ-2 name Lakshmi. UJ-3 to UJ-5 use "the owner", acceptable for a single operator. UJ-6 has "A shopper", unnamed; reuse Lakshmi.
- **Sections.** §4.10 has no Description line, unlike §4.1 to §4.9. All sections expected for launch stakes are present. Referenced brief and research folders exist.
- **Addendum versus PRD.** The addendum's FR-19 note ("reserves stock when the order is placed") conflicts with the Glossary definition of Order; see the critical finding.
